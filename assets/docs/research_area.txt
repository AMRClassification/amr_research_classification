Research Area

1. Basic Research

Focus: Fundamental understanding of diseases, immune responses, or pathogens not directed toward a specific product.
Stages: Technology Readiness Levels (TRL) 1-3.
Includes:
Studies on pathogen mechanisms, transmission, virulence, immunology, genetics, microbiome roles, and One Health interactions.
Development of models and technologies assisting in drug and vaccine design and testing.

2. Therapeutics

Focus: Treatment of infections using antimicrobials.
Includes:
Improvement of existing antimicrobials and therapies.
Combination therapies and dose optimization.
Development of new antimicrobials and alternatives (e.g., antibodies, probiotics, bacteriophages).

3. Vaccines

Focus: Products stimulating the adaptive immune system to prevent disease.
Includes:
Research on vaccine development challenges (e.g., antigen identification, delivery methods).
Identification and assessment of vaccine candidates for safety and efficacy.

4. Preventives - Other

Focus: Non-vaccine products preventing disease without inducing specific immune memory.
Includes:
Prophylactic treatments.
Immune modulators enhancing general immune function.
Breeding for disease resistance (e.g., genome editing).
Anti-biofilm agents.

5. Diagnostics

Focus: Development or improvement of detection, screening, or diagnostic tools.
Includes:
Identification of causative agents and resistance profiles.
Diagnostic tests for clinical and field use.
Tools for surveillance and epidemiological studies.
Companion diagnostics and machine learning detection tools.

6. Promotants

Focus: Products improving health or productivity in the absence of disease.
Includes:
Probiotics and prebiotics.
Antimicrobial peptides and phytochemicals.
Organic acids, enzymes, and other growth-promoting alternatives.

7. Other Products

Focus: External or topical applications not classified elsewhere.
Includes:
Biocides (antiseptics, disinfectants).
Biofilm prevention materials.
Medical devices and wound care products.
Health monitoring technologies (e.g., sensors in animals).

8. Operational Research

Focus: Aiding decision-making and management strategies.
Includes:
Infection prevention and control practices.
Antimicrobial stewardship and optimal use studies.
Access and availability improvements for AMR technologies.
Surveillance and epidemiological studies.
Social science research on behavior change and economic analyses.

9. Capacity Building

Focus: Enhancing human and infrastructural resources to tackle AMR.
Includes:
Laboratory capacity enhancement.
Training and staff development.
Network formation for knowledge sharing.
Process improvements beyond single products.

10. Policy

Focus: Informing policy and regulation development or revision.
Includes:
Research proposing policy changes or stakeholder action.
Studies on healthcare services impact, economic models, and health technology assessments.



Product Development Stages

1. Discovery

The discovery and preclinical testing of innovative methods, processes, active ingredients, antigens, adjuvants, delivery vehicles/methods, diagnostics and corresponds to ‘Technology Readiness Levels’ 4-5. Several in vitro and in vivo methods are applied in order to assess biological activity, immunogenicity, efficacy and safety (toxicological studies) of potential candidates. The preclinical phase concludes with submission of an IND (Investigational New Drug) application with FDA-US or CTA (Clinical Trial Application) EMA-EU by submitting the IMPD (Investigational Medicinal Product Dossier).
• For therapeutics and drug preventives this includes target validation, the hit discovery process (hit identification, hit to lead, lead identification and optimisation)
• For preventative biologics such as vaccines this includes identification, selection and improvement/characterisation of vaccine components (antigen, adjuvant, carrier/delivery system, etc) that have the ability to induce immunogenicity (induction of cellular and humoral immune response/adaptive immune responses). This may include testing of serum in order to identify antigens/immunogens (samples from exposed individuals for testing immunogenicity), testing for ability to induce protective immune responses (e.g. by challenge with pathogen in animal or human models)
• For diagnostics, this includes concept, feasibility, prototype development, and development of technical specifications
• In Animal Health, includes market assessment to identify unmet animal health needs, preclinical/feasibility studies, including proof-of-concept safety and efficacy studies, and are usually conducted in target species
• In plants, this includes treatment concepts and other control strategies


2. Development

The progression of selected candidates from development to commercialisation including investigating the efficacy and safety of the product in the field (e.g. clinical trials under GCP conditions), reformulation and repurposing and validation of manufacturing processes. This stage concludes with submission of an NDA (New Drug Application) or NADA (New Animal Drug Application) with the FDA/CVM or appropriate applications/dossiers with agencies in other countries and corresponds to ‘Technology Readiness Levels’ 6-8.• In Human Health, for therapeutics and preventives this includes clinical trial Phase 1 to Phase 3, and trials that will lead to an expansion of the product label (additional indications)
• In Animal Health, Target Animal Safety Studies (TASS) and Target Animal Effectiveness Studies (TAES) are conducted and include dose-finding and field trials (designed to mimic its ‘everyday’ use)
• For diagnostics this includes design lock, validation of manufacturing process, validation of accuracy and analytical performance in (clinical) trials, validation of performance and operational characteristics during uncontrolled routine use in programmatic settings

3. Approval and Post-Approval

Refers to the phase following first market authorisation (early-commercialisation) for a specific product and corresponds to ‘Technology Readiness Levels’ 9. This could include but is not limited to:
• Filing in other, subsequent, legal jurisdictions (countries)
• All subsequent research and monitoring that is a requirement by regulators (post-approval
requirements or post-authorisation obligations), such as: paediatric investigation plans
(PIPs), pharmacovigilance (phase 4) etc
• Research into product optimisation (such as bioavailability, formulations)
• Clinical studies which help inform how a product should be used in a clinical setting to
inform among others product formulary inclusion, guideline incorporation, value- assessment and payor decisions




Technology Readiness Levels (TRL)

TRL 1: Basic principles observed.
TRL 2: Concept formulated.
TRL 3: Experimental proof of concept.
TRL 4: Technology validated in the lab.
TRL 5: Technology validated in a relevant environment.
TRL 6: Technology demonstrated in a relevant environment.
TRL 7: System prototype in an operational environment.
TRL 8: System complete and qualified.
TRL 9: Actual system proven in operational environment.
