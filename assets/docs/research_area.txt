Research Area


1.	Basic Research

•	Focus: Fundamental understanding of diseases, immune responses, or pathogens not directed toward a specific product.
•	Stages: Technology Readiness Levels (TRL) 1-3.
•	Includes:

•	Sub-categories:


a)	Fundamental - no clear path to product development (TLR 1-2)
•	 Studies on pathogen mechanisms, transmission, virulence, immunology, genetics, microbiome roles, and One Health interactions involving humans, animals, and the environment. 

b)	Towards a product - has the potential to become a product (TLR 3)
•	Search for potential therapeutic, preventive, promotants or diagnostic target
•	Early research for the development of imaging or detection technologies/assays
•	Models and technologies assisting in drug and vaccine design and testing.
•	Identification of mode of action of putative new products
•	Platform technologies for vaccines 



2.	Therapeutics

•	Focus: Product-specific R&D for treatment of infections using antimicrobials.
•	Includes:
•	Improvement of existing antimicrobials and therapies.
•	Combination therapies and dose optimization studies.
•	Development of new antimicrobials and alternatives (e.g., antibodies, probiotics, faecal transplant therapy, bacteriophages, antimicrobial peptides, lysins, antitoxins and immune modulators).
•	Investigation of old or off market antimicrobials
•	Drug quality, bioavailability, stability

•	Sub-categories:
a)	Discovery-discovery and preclinical testing of innovative methods
•	Target assessment & validation, the hit discovery process (hit identification, hit to lead, lead identification, lead optimisation)
•	Preclinical development – ends with submission of an IND (Investigational New Drug) application

b)	Clinical Testing – Clinical trials phase 1- phase 3
•	Clinical Studies/Trials Phase 1 to Phase 3, also includes trials that will lead to an expansion of the product label or additional indications (diseases/infections)
•	This stage concludes with submission of an NDA (New Drug Application)

c)	Approval/Post Approval - 
•	First market authorisation (early-commercialisation) for a specific product
•	All subsequent research and monitoring required by regulators (post-approval requirements or post-authorisation obligations), pharmacovigilance (phase 4)
•	Studies how a product should be used in a clinical setting to inform among others product formulary inclusion, guideline incorporation, value assessment and payor decisions
•	Approved product optimisation (such as bioavailability, formulations)
•	How a product should be used in a clinical setting to inform among others product formulary inclusion, guideline incorporation, value-assessment and payor decisions



3.	Vaccines

•	Focus: Products stimulating the adaptive immune system to prevent disease.
•	Includes:
•	Research on vaccine development challenges (e.g., antigen identification, delivery methods).
•	Identification and assessment of vaccine candidates for safety and eﬃcacy.

•	Sub-categories:
a)	Discovery
•	identification, selection and improvement/characterisation of vaccine components (antigen, adjuvant, carrier/delivery system, etc) that have the ability to induce immunogenicity (induction of cellular and humoral immune response/adaptive immune responses)
•	testing of serum in order to identify antigens/immunogens (samples from exposed individuals for testing immunogenicity), testing for ability to induce protective immune responses (e.g. by challenge with pathogen in animal or human models)
b)	Clinical Testing
•	Clinical Studies/Trials Phase 1 to Phase 3, also includes trials that will lead to an expansion of the product label or additional indications (diseases/infections)
•	This stage concludes with submission of an NDA (New Drug Application)

c)	Approval/Post Approval
•	First market authorisation (early-commercialisation) for a specific product
•	All subsequent research and monitoring required by regulators (post-approval requirements or post-authorisation obligations), pharmacovigilance (phase 4)
•	Studies how a product should be used in a clinical setting to inform among others product formulary inclusion, guideline incorporation, value assessment and payor decisions
•	Approved product optimisation (such as bioavailability, formulations)
•	How a product should be used in a clinical setting to inform among others product formulary inclusion, guideline incorporation, value-assessment and payor decisions



4.	Preventives - Other

•	Focus: Non-vaccine products preventing disease without inducing specific immune memory.
•	Includes:
•	Prophylactic treatments.
•	Immune modulators enhancing general immune function.
•	Breeding for disease resistance (e.g., genome editing).
•	Anti-biofilm agents.

• Sub-categories:
a)	Discovery
•	identification, selection and improvement/characterisation of product
b)	Clinical Testing
•	clinical trial Phase 1 to Phase 3, also includes trials that will lead to an expansion of the product label (additional indications)
•	This stage concludes with submission of an NDA (New Drug Application)
c)	Approval/Post Approval
•	First market authorization (early-commercialisation) for a specific product
•	All subsequent research and monitoring required by regulators (post-approval requirements or post-authorisation obligations), pharmacovigilance (phase 4)
•	Studies how a product should be used in a clinical setting to inform among and payor decisions
•	Approved product optimisation (such as bioavailability, formulations)
•	How a product should be used in a clinical setting to inform among others product formulary inclusion, guideline incorporation, value-assessment and payor decisions


5.	Diagnostics

•	Focus: Development or improvement of detection, screening, or diagnostic tools.
•	Includes:
•	Identification of causative agents and resistance profiles.
•	Diagnostic tests for clinical and field use.
•	Tools for surveillance and epidemiological studies.
•	Companion diagnostics and machine learning detection tools.


• Sub-categories:
a)	Discovery
•	concept, feasibility, prototype development, and development of technical specifications
b)	Clinical Testing
•	design lock, validation of manufacturing process, validation of accuracy and analytical performance in (clinical) trials, validation of performance and operational characteristics during uncontrolled routine use in programmatic settings
c)	Approval/Post Approval
•	First market authorisation (early-commercialisation) for a specific product
•	All subsequent research and monitoring required by regulators (post-approval requirements or post-authorisation obligations), pharmacovigilance (phase 4)
•	Studies how a product should be used in a clinical setting to inform among others product formulary inclusion, guideline incorporation, value assessment and payor decisions
•	Approved product optimisation (such as bioavailability, formulations)
•	How a product should be used in a clinical setting to inform among others product formulary inclusion, guideline incorporation, value-assessment and payor decisions


 
 
6.	Promotants

•	Focus: Products improving health or productivity in the absence of disease.
•	Includes:
•	Probiotics and prebiotics.
•	Antimicrobial peptides and phytochemicals.
•	Organic acids, enzymes, and other growth-promoting alternatives.


7.	Other Products

•	Focus: External or topical applications not classified elsewhere.
•	Includes:
•	Biocides (antiseptics, disinfectants).
•	Biofilm prevention materials.
•	Medical devices and wound care products.
•	Health monitoring technologies (e.g., sensors in animals).


8.	Operational Research

•	Focus: Aiding decision-making and management strategies.
•	Includes:
•	Infection prevention and control practices.
•	Antimicrobial stewardship and optimal use studies.
•	Access and availability improvements for AMR technologies.
•	Surveillance and epidemiological studies.
•	Social science research on behavior change and economic analyses.


9.	Capacity Building

•	Focus: Enhancing human and infrastructural resources to tackle AMR.
•	Includes:
•	Laboratory capacity enhancement.
•	Training and staﬀ development.
•	Network formation for knowledge sharing.
•	Process improvements beyond single products.


10.	Policy

•	Focus: Informing policy and regulation development or revision.
•	Includes:
•	Research proposing policy changes or stakeholder action.
•	Studies on healthcare services impact, economic models, and health technology assessments.
 
Product Development Stages
( for Therapeutics, Diagnostics, Vaccines, Preventives-Other)


1.	Discovery

•	TRL: 4-5.
•	Includes:
•	Target validation and hit discovery for therapeutics and preventives.
•	Vaccine component identification and improvement.
•	Diagnostic concept development and prototype creation.
•	Preclinical trials


2.	Clinical Testing

•	TRL: 6-8.
•	Includes:
•	Clinical trials (Phases 1-3) for human therapeutics and preventives.
•	Safety and eﬀectiveness studies for animal health products.
•	Validation of manufacturing processes and product performance.


3.	Approval and Post-Approval

•	TRL: 9.
•	Includes:
•	Regulatory filings in various jurisdictions.
•	Post-approval obligations (e.g., pharmacovigilance).
•	Product optimization research.
•	Clinical studies informing product use and guidelines.




Technology Readiness Levels (TRL)
•	TRL 1: Basic principles observed.
•	TRL 2: Concept formulated.
•	TRL 3: Experimental proof of concept.
•	TRL 4: Technology validated in the lab.
•	TRL 5: Technology validated in a relevant environment.
•	TRL 6: Technology demonstrated in a relevant environment.
•	TRL 7: System prototype in an operational environment.
•	TRL 8: System complete and qualified.
•	TRL 9: Actual system proven in operational environment.
